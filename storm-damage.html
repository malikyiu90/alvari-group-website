Storm damage and emergency roofing

We answer the phone 24 hours a day. If your roof has opened up tonight, the first job is to stop more water coming in — the rest can wait until daylight.

Get a free estimate Call 07483 172469

Storm work splits into two halves. Make-safe happens now: sheeting over, removing anything that could fall, and getting the property watertight. The permanent repair happens once we can see what we are dealing with and, where an insurer is involved, once the claim is agreed.

What happens when you call

  1. We ask what you can see and whether anyone is at risk. If there is a danger to people below, that is a 999 call first.
  2. We attend and make the property safe — sheeting, removing loose material, isolating the area.
  3. We photograph everything, which is what your insurer will want.
  4. We give you a written quote for the permanent repair, itemised so the insurer can assess it.

Insurance work

Most storm damage is covered. Insurers normally pay for make-safe work carried out urgently, provided it is documented — which is why we photograph before we touch anything.

We deal with loss adjusters regularly and will talk to yours directly if you would rather we did. What we will not do is inflate a quote because an insurer is paying.

Frequently asked

Will my insurance cover it?

Storm damage usually is covered; wear and tear is not. The distinction turns on evidence, which is why we photograph the roof before any work.

Do you charge extra at night?

Emergency attendance is priced in the quote and you are told the figure before we come out. There is no surprise call-out charge.

How quickly can you get here?

For a property taking water in we aim for same day. Scaffold for the permanent repair takes longer to arrange.

Signs you need this

  • Tiles or slates off and water coming in now
  • Part of the roof visibly lifted or displaced
  • A chimney or ridge that has moved
  • A fallen tree or branch on the roof
  • Lead flashing peeled back by wind
  • Fence panels and debris suggesting a localised gust
